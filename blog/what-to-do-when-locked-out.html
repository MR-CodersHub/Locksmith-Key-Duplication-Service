What to Do When You Are Locked Out

Finding yourself locked out is stressful, disorienting, and frustrating. Here is the calm, step-by-step master locksmith protocol to resolve the situation without damaging your home.

Marcus Vance Master Guild Technician
Published: September 15, 2026 • 5 Min Read • ← Back to Journal

1. Stay Calm and Assess the Situation

The immediate psychological reaction to a lockout is an adrenaline surge accompanied by frantic door shaking. Stop, take a deep breath, and assess the immediate safety parameters:

  • Is there an immediate hazard inside (e.g. stove burner on, bath filling, infant unattended)? If so, call 911 emergency services immediately.
  • Are you stranded in extreme cold, darkness, or an unsafe area? Seek a lit public venue or neighbor's foyer before making phone calls.
“A five-minute pause saves hundreds of dollars in broken window glass and splintered door trim.”

2. Safely Verify Secondary Entries

Before calling for emergency dispatch, calmly walk the perimeter of the residence:

Is a ground-floor kitchen or sunroom window unlocked? Is a sliding patio door latch loose? Can an attached garage be opened via a keypad or mobile app? Often, an alternative entry point is accessible. Never, however, climb onto perilous second-story roofs or squeeze into window wells where you could become trapped.

3. What NOT to Do (Avoid Costly Damage)

Hollywood movies make lock picking look trivial using credit cards or bobby pins. In real life, modern deadbolts feature deadlocking pins that physically block credit card slips.

Jamming knives, hairpins, or screwdrivers into modern lock cylinders will bend the keyway brass, mangle the spring chambers, or snap metal inside the plug. What could have been a rapid $90 non-destructive entry now requires destructive drilling, cylinder replacement, and door repainting that easily runs $300+.

4. Spotting Emergency Locksmith Scams

The emergency locksmith trade is rife with deceptive lead-generation call centers. They run ads advertising absurdly cheap service fees like "$15 Service Call".

When an unbadged driver arrives in an unmarked car, they immediately claim your lock is "high security," pull out a power drill, destroy your hardware, and demand $400 to $800 in cash.

How to protect yourself: Always call a local, accredited company with a verified physical workshop address. Demand an upfront, fixed-estimate quote over the phone that includes dispatch, labor, and hardware, and verify guild licensing (#LK-89412) upon arrival.

5. Understanding Non-Destructive Entry

At Keyora, over 95% of residential and commercial lockouts are opened non-destructively.

Our guild technicians carry specialized precision lock picks, decoding scopes, plug spinners, and bypass tools designed to decode the internal shear line and turn the plug without a single scratch to your door paint or hardware finish. Your existing keys will work perfectly the moment we finish.

6. Modern Protocols to Prevent the Next Lockout

Once you are safely inside, take concrete steps to ensure this never happens again:

  1. Have duplicate spare keys laser cut and stored with a trusted neighbor.
  2. Install a heavy-duty mechanical push-button key vault in a discreet location.
  3. Consider upgrading to an ANSI Grade 1 smart deadbolt with an exterior keypad and biometric fingerprint sensor.

Keyora Guild Takeaways

  • Never attempt to force locks with credit cards, paper clips, or screwdrivers—you will destroy the cylinder.
  • Beware of scam locksmiths quoting $15–$29 online; legitimate master locksmiths provide clear fixed pricing upfront.
  • Licensed guild locksmiths utilize non-destructive picking and bypass techniques that preserve your lock and door.
  • Always request photo identification and licensing credentials before allowing a technician to touch your lock.
