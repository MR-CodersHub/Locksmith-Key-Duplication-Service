10 Practical Ways to Make Your Home More Secure

True residential security is not about transforming your home into a windowless fortress—it is about systematically eliminating low-effort vulnerabilities that burglars exploit in seconds.

Marcus Vance Master Guild Technician (18+ Yrs Experience)
Published: October 14, 2026 • 8 Min Read • ← Back to Journal

1. Deadbolt Throw Depth & ANSI Ratings

Most residential builder-grade deadbolts are rated ANSI Grade 3. While they look substantial from the street, their internal components are often stamped pot metal or hollow zinc alloy. When subjected to a standard kick-in attempt, the bolt mechanism frequently sheers inside the cylinder housing.

For true physical protection, insist on an ANSI Grade 1 mechanical deadbolt. Grade 1 specifications mandate a minimum of 250,000 operational cycles, a solid 1-inch hardened steel throw with a free-spinning internal roller pin (which prevents hacksaws from cutting through the bolt), and high-tensile housing screws.

“A lock is only as strong as the door jamb it engages. If your bolt extends into flimsy pine trim, an intruder isn't picking your lock—they are splintering the door frame in three seconds flat.”

2. 3-Inch Hardened Strike Screws

When carpenters install residential door frames, they invariably use 3/4-inch screws to fasten the brass strike plate. These tiny screws only bite into the decorative soft pine door trim, which has virtually zero structural resistance against a blunt kick.

Remove those factory screws immediately and replace them with 3-inch #8 or #9 case-hardened steel screws. These longer fasteners bridge the decorative casing, penetrate the hollow gap behind the frame, and bite at least two inches deep into the structural 2x4 framing studs of the home. This single five-dollar upgrade increases kick resistance by more than 400 percent.

3. Bump-Proof & Pick-Resistant Cylinders

Lock bumping is a covert entry technique that uses an inexpensive, specially cut key and kinetic tapping to manipulate pin tumblers simultaneously. Standard retail lock cylinders possess zero anti-bump countermeasures.

Consider upgrading your core cylinders to high-security platforms like Medeco, Mul-T-Lock, or Schlage Primus. These commercial-grade cylinders feature spool, serrated, and mushroom security top pins that catch when manipulated, alongside patented secondary sidebars that demand precise rotational angle alignment before the plug will rotate.

4. Sliding Patio & French Door Anchors

Patio sliding doors are notorious primary points of vulnerability. Their factory latch mechanisms are often simple aluminum hooks that can be popped upward with a flat pry bar in under five seconds. Furthermore, older sliding panels can frequently be lifted right out of their bottom tracks.

Secure sliding doors with a heavy-gauge adjustable steel security bar wedged into the bottom track, or install an auxiliary keyed pin lock through the upper door stiles. For French double doors, replace standard surface slide bolts with heavy-duty flush mortise bolts that extend deep into both the upper door header and the concrete threshold below.

5. Restricted Keyways & Key Control

Standard hardware store keys can be duplicated in sixty seconds at any grocery store automated kiosk without proof of ownership or authorization. If a contractor, cleaner, or previous resident ever held your key for an afternoon, duplicate copies may be floating in circulation.

A restricted keyway system utilizes patented key blanks that hardware stores and kiosks cannot legally or physically cut. Only your designated master locksmith can cut duplicates upon presentation of an authorized identity signature card.

6. Strategic Perimeter Illumination

Burglars crave concealment. Shadows cast by overgrown shrubs around recessed front entryways provide ideal concealment while tampering with hardware.

Install downward-focused warm LED pathway and soffit lighting coupled with motion-activated security fixtures covering side passages and rear entrances. Keep all foundation hedges trimmed below windowsill height to maintain clear sightlines from the street.

7. Hardening Side & Garage Entrances

Homeowners frequently invest heavily in an ornate front door deadbolt, while leaving the detached garage service door or basement bulkhead fitted with a flimsy bedroom-grade key-in-knob lock.

Statistically, over 65% of unlawful entries occur through rear or garage access points. Every exterior door must adhere to the exact same security standard: solid-core construction, Grade 1 deadbolt, reinforced strike box, and heavy-duty latch guards.

8. Balancing Smart Locks with Mechanics

Keyless touchpads and smartphone access provide unmatched convenience for families and visiting guests. However, battery depletion, Wi-Fi outages, and electronic motor strain mean that electronic locks must always be paired with a robust mechanical override.

When selecting a smart lock, verify that it retains a physical key cylinder with high-security pick resistance rather than a cheap toy-grade override wafer.

9. Seasonal Mechanical Lubrication

A dry, sticking cylinder forces you to torque the key blade with excessive leverage, eventually bending the brass shoulder or snapping the key inside the plug.

Twice a year—preferably at the start of spring and winter—blow a small puff of dry micro-fine graphite powder or specialized PTFE aerosol into the keyway and exercise the key 5–10 times. Avoid oil-based fluids, which turn into thick grime that seizes internal tumbler springs.

10. Professional Security Walkthrough

Even vigilant homeowners have blind spots. A licensed master locksmith can perform an on-site physical security audit to inspect door frame plumbness, hinge reveal gaps, strike alignment, and perimeter lighting.

Keyora Guild Takeaways

  • Upgrade standard deadbolts to ANSI/BHMA Grade 1 with a full 1-inch hardened steel throw bolt.
  • Replace all factory 3/4-inch strike plate screws with 3-inch hardened screws anchored into wall studs.
  • Sliding glass doors require top-track security bars or auxiliary pin locks to prevent track lifting.
  • Never spray WD-40 or oil into pin tumbler cylinders; use micro-fine dry graphite only.
